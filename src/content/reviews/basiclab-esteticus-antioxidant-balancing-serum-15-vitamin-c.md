### What Is BasicLab Esteticus Antioxidant Balancing Serum?

[BasicLab Esteticus Antioxidant Balancing Serum](https://basiclab.shop/pl/products/antyoksydacyjne-serum-wyrownujace-z-wit-c-15-rozswietlenie-i-wyciszenie-36) is a fragrance-free, water-gel serum made with 15% 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid. That is a stabilized vitamin C derivative rather than pure L-ascorbic acid, so the formula is designed to be gentler and less temperamental while still targeting dullness, uneven tone, and dark spots.

Rice-water ferment filtrate and a prebiotic sit alongside glutathione, resveratrol, vitamin E, cucumber extract, and willowherb. BasicLab recommends it for normal, combination, oily, acne-prone, and sensitive skin. The 30 ml bottle is currently £35.49 at Vesa; in Poland, BasicLab lists it at 169.90 zł before promotions.

### My Thoughts on the BasicLab Vitamin C Serum

Poland has incredible drogerie, where drugstore meets pharmacy. Super-Pharm is one of my favorites, along with Rossmann. I found this BasicLab vitamin C serum there on sale and decided to try it. This is how many of my Polish skincare discoveries begin: I walk in for one thing and leave with an ingredient to investigate.

The serum has no noticeable scent and causes no irritation on my skin. It feels light, spreads easily, and disappears without a tacky film. I use it with [ACM Azéane Cream 15% Azelaic Acid](https://www.caretobeauty.com/us/acm-laboratoire-azeane-cream-15-azelaic-acid-30ml/) without any layering issues. I always apply from lightest to thickest, so BasicLab goes first, followed by the cream and then SPF.

I keep the bottle in the fridge because vitamin C has a reputation for instability. This is not a classic, easily oxidized L-ascorbic acid formula, though. BasicLab uses a stable ethylated derivative and recommends a dry, dark place at room temperature, with a six-month period after opening. Refrigeration is my extra-cautious habit, especially during the summer, but not a requirement.

What impresses me most is how undramatic it is. It does not sting, smell metallic, pill, or force me to rearrange my routine. Vitamin C is usually a long-game ingredient, and this is easy to use consistently. BasicLab is an underrated Polish brand, and now I want to investigate the rest of the shelf.

### My Verdict

This is a yes for me, particularly on sale in Poland. It gives me a meaningful concentration of stable vitamin C without irritation and layers beautifully with azelaic acid. I would recommend it if acidic vitamin C serums feel too fussy or stingy. At import prices, the alternatives below are easier to justify.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### Does This Vitamin C Serum Need to Be Refrigerated?

No. BasicLab says to store it somewhere dry and dark at room temperature and use it within six months of opening. I keep mine in the fridge as an extra precaution.

#### Can You Use This Serum with Azelaic Acid?

I comfortably layer it before ACM Azéane in the morning. Both can irritate some skin, so introduce them one at a time and separate them if your skin objects.

#### Is BasicLab's Vitamin C Serum Pregnancy Safe?

BasicLab explicitly says the serum can be used during pregnancy and breastfeeding. That supports the pregnancy-safe label here, but your clinician should still review your complete routine if you have questions.
