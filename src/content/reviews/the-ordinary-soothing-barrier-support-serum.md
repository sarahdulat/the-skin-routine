### What Is The Ordinary Soothing & Barrier Support Serum?

[The Ordinary Soothing & Barrier Support Serum](https://theordinary.com/en-us/soothing-barrier-support-serum-100634.html) is a $17 emulsion-serum for dryness, visible redness, discomfort, and a damaged skin barrier. Its eight technologies include 2% niacinamide, ceramides, vitamin B12, centella compounds, bisabolol, sugar-based hydrators, gallic acid derivatives, and a soothing complex.

Vitamin B12 gives the formula its wonderfully unserious Pepto-Bismol pink color. The brand says it can strengthen the barrier within two hours and immediately increase hydration by 86%, although those claims come from small panels of 23 and 34 people. Apply it after cleansing and before heavier creams or oils.

### My Thoughts on The Ordinary Soothing & Barrier Support Serum

The color is easily the most memorable thing about this serum. It is properly pink, not vaguely blush if you hold it up to the light. The smell is a little strange, but not strong or off-putting enough to keep me from using it.

My issue is that when my barrier actually needs the promised soothing and support, this serum tends to sting upon application. That left me wondering whether I was using it wrong—perhaps it was meant to be preventative, and I was introducing it after my barrier was already shot.

According to The Ordinary, though, this is specifically formulated for damaged and compromised barriers. The brand also says to use it only on unbroken skin and to rinse it off and stop if irritation occurs. In other words, I was not misunderstanding its purpose, and stinging is not proof that it is doing something useful. A compromised barrier can make even normally gentle products sting, but that does not mean I need to power through it.

When my skin is calm, the serum applies easily and disappears into the routine. Unfortunately, it also disappears from my memory. I cannot point to a soothing feeling or a visible difference that makes me excited to reach for it. For all the very impressive promises on paper, I would rather use a barrier-repairing moisturizer that adds comfort and seals in moisture in one step.

### What the Internet Thinks about The Ordinary Barrier Serum

The internet is sharply divided. [Ulta](https://www.ulta.com/p/soothing-barrier-support-serum-sensitive-skin-hydration-pimprod2041026?sku=2615437) shows a 4.3-star average from more than 1,300 reviews, with fans praising softer, calmer skin. One [Reddit reviewer](https://www.reddit.com/r/SkincareAddiction/comments/1fbgmiy/review_just_started_my_third_bottle_of_the/) reached a third bottle and said it kept flaking under control while using actives.

The critical experiences sound familiar to me. Ulta reviewers mention stinging, a strange smell, increased redness, and rash-like breakouts. In another [Reddit review](https://www.reddit.com/r/SkincareAddiction/comments/1be5pln/review_the_ordinary_ordinary_soothing_barrier/), a user with rosacea and dry skin reported burning and small pustules. That does not make the serum universally irritating, but it makes patch testing especially sensible when skin is already reactive.

### Would I Repurchase The Ordinary Soothing & Barrier Support Serum?

No. It is inexpensive and has a thoughtful ingredient list, but a soothing product that stings when I need soothing most is not earning permanent shelf space. It may make more sense as everyday maintenance for someone whose skin already tolerates it. For an actively angry barrier, I would simplify the routine and choose a bland, comforting moisturizer instead.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### Is This Serum Only for Preventative Barrier Support?

No. The Ordinary explicitly positions it for damaged and compromised barriers. If it burns or makes redness worse, follow the brand's directions: rinse it off and stop using it rather than waiting for your skin to “adjust.”

#### Why Is The Ordinary Barrier Serum Pink?

The color comes from vitamin B12, also listed as cyanocobalamin. It is part of the formula rather than added pink dye.

#### Is The Ordinary Soothing & Barrier Support Serum Pregnancy Safe?

The formula contains no retinoid, and The Ordinary only specifically advises avoiding its retinoid products during pregnancy and breastfeeding. However, the brand says its products have not been tested in pregnant or breastfeeding people, so I have marked this one as unverified rather than unsafe. Check with your clinician if you want to add it during pregnancy.
